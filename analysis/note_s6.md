## Note S6. Protocol history, reading rules and notes on the external baselines (moved from the main text)

The following passages stood in sections 3.4 and 3.3 of earlier versions of the manuscript and are given here unchanged; section and table numbers are those of the main text, citation numbers those of its reference list.

Fixing hypotheses and decision rules before an experiment limits analytic flexibility [53]. Each case followed a protocol written before its main experiment (Supplementary Note S1; archived publicly after the experiments, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24269036; the archive fixes the content but does not prove the time of writing). The case-1 C-MAPSS and N-CMAPSS protocols were created at 03:24 and 03:41 UTC, before the first results at 05:02 UTC on the same day. The case-2 protocol preceded its runs except for one run-through check whose results were not inspected; an appendix adding S6 and S8 was written after the C-MAPSS results were known, while the N-CMAPSS runs were in progress (per-unit numbers of two folds of one seed had been seen). A third case-1 protocol, for A8 and GRU-D on N-CMAPSS, was written two minutes after a first run had been started and stopped, once the results of one fold had been seen; all folds were then rerun unchanged. Pilot studies had used the official C-MAPSS test sets in both cases, so confirmation rests on N-CMAPSS.

In both cases, H1 states that the proposed arm has lower error than the better test-time remedy under incompleteness (case 1: min(A1, A2, A3) at k = 5 and irregular 1–5; case 2: min(S1, S2) with the last 10 and 5 cycles on C-MAPSS and the last 5 and 3 on N-CMAPSS), tested with Holm correction over 8 C-MAPSS and 2 N-CMAPSS comparisons. H2 states that the proposed arm is not worse than the dense model with complete records. In case 1, H2 required only non-significance, a protocol rule of no detected difference rather than a non-inferiority test; in case 2, it also required the upper bound of the 95% bootstrap interval of the difference to be below 1.0 cycle. The C-MAPSS criteria required at least 7 of 8 H1 comparisons and H2 on all four subsets; the N-CMAPSS criteria required both H1 comparisons and H2. The appendix and third protocol specified the same H1 and H2 for S8 and A8, with a 1.0-cycle non-inferiority margin for A8 against A5. The margin was chosen as about 13% of A5's RMSE without an engineering derivation and was applied to mean absolute error; we therefore report the intervals in full (Table S17) so that other bounds can be applied. The protocols also listed secondary hypotheses (case 1: gap channel, A5 vs A4; history length with complete records, A5 vs A6; interval-specific models, A5 vs A7; case 2: indicator channel, S4 vs S3; length-specific models, S4 vs S5); they are reported in sections 4.2 and 4.5 as uncorrected results (Supplementary Note S3).

A further protocol, written and checksummed before its runs, covers the external cohort, the missingness scenarios not seen in training and the padding experiment (sections 4.8–4.10). On the external cohort it repeats H1 for both cases as confirmatory hypotheses (E1: A5 against the best of A1, A2, A3 at k = 5 and irregular 1–5; E2: S4 against the better of S1, S2 with the last 5 and 3 cycles; Holm within each pair) and reports the complete-record cost with a 1-cycle non-inferiority bound (E3). For the new missingness scenarios it fixed the reading that degraded training remains better if an arm is Holm-significantly better than the best remedy in at least six of the eight C-MAPSS block comparisons and in both external-cohort block comparisons. For the padding experiment it fixed the prediction that over-estimation near end of life is ordered S3E > S3 > S3L, supported if the difference between S3E and S3L is Holm-significant in at least six of eight C-MAPSS comparisons and in both external-cohort comparisons. A last protocol, also checksummed before its runs, covers the two other backbones: for each backbone, A5 against the best remedy (G1), A8 against A6 (G2) and S4 against the better padding remedy (G3) are tested as before, and an effect is said to carry over if the degraded-training arm is Holm-significantly better in at least six of the eight C-MAPSS comparisons and in both external-cohort comparisons, and to carry over partly if only one of the two conditions holds. A further protocol, checksummed before its runs, asked three questions with the GRU (section 4.12): whether the deployment-like gap law adds anything to generic random deletion at about the same density (reading: "exposure to deletion is enough" if generic deletion is significantly better than dense training in at least six of eight C-MAPSS comparisons and the gap law is significantly better than generic deletion in at most two); with what probability r complete windows should be added to the training set of the gap-channel model, with r chosen on validation units by the mean of the validation RMSE with complete records and at k = 5; and whether a model trained on smoothed but complete windows reproduces the gain. The first analysis of the second question used unadjusted p values and took the best remedy among A1 and A2 only; after an independent check, the comparisons were Holm-adjusted as the protocol requires and carry-forward (A3) was included among the remedies, without retraining (Table S32). The readings did not change. A last protocol, checksummed before its runs, covers a missingness simulation on real degradation records of lithium-ion cells (section 4.13); its three readings (gain with sparse records, gain under an outage not seen in training, and the rule for adding complete windows) and their non-inferiority margin are given there.

The case-2 dense models S1 and S6 were trained with the same code, seeds and hardware as A1 and A6 of the five-seed case-1 run, and their predictions are identical; they are the same models evaluated on a different scenario. The supervised imputer replaced a GAIN-style imputer [52] whose imputation error under regular spacing was 5–7 times that of linear interpolation; a later check found that its hint mechanism deviated from [52], so this failure reflects our implementation and is not evidence about GAIN (Supplementary Note S2). GRU-D is an adaptation of [28] to whole-record sparsity: every record is complete, so the missingness mask is constant, and the record decayed towards the training mean and the gap are supplied as extra inputs next to the current record, with hidden-state decay as in [28]; it is not the original formulation and is reported as a GRU-D-style model. Our re-implementation of the parametric rectification of [35] degraded accuracy as spacing grew, so SNAP is reported without it and is not a test of that method. PC-MambaSDE [34] was run from the authors' released code, with one class-name clash corrected so that the described Mamba encoder is used, and given the elapsed time since the window start as its time input. It received the same number of epochs and batch size as the other arms (30 epochs, batch size 256; we did not equalise wall-clock time or computation), which corresponds to about one twentieth of the gradient steps of the authors' default; it was not tuned, and we did not adopt the released code's selection of checkpoints by test-set error. Its irregularity setting in [34] (asynchronous per-sensor dropout and burst missingness on a fixed grid) also differs from ours (whole records missing at unequal intervals).
